<p class="lede">Most growing businesses build their online presence one piece at a time. A logo from a freelance designer, a website from a developer, a video from a production house, and recently a chatbot from someone else again. Each piece may be well made. The trouble is in the gaps between them.</p>

## The hidden cost of separate vendors

When five suppliers each own one part, nobody owns the whole result. The owner, or a busy manager, becomes the person who passes files from one vendor to another, explains the brand again, and settles disagreements about whose job a problem is.

These costs rarely appear on an invoice, but they are real:

- Time spent coordinating instead of serving customers.
- A brand that looks slightly different on the website, the videos and social media.
- Enquiries that arrive through a chatbot but never reach the sales team.
- Delays while one vendor waits for another to finish.

> A customer does not see five vendors. They see one business, and they judge it by the weakest piece.

## What changes with one connected team

With one team, the work is planned together from the first conversation. The brand designer knows which pages the website needs. The web developer knows where the promo video will sit and where the assistant will answer questions. The person setting up the chatbot knows which system the leads should go to.

### Decisions are made once

Colours, tone of voice, key messages and priorities are agreed at the start and carried into every piece. You do not repeat the same brief to five people.

### One person is accountable

You have one named contact. If something breaks after launch, there is no debate about whose fault it is. The same team that built it fixes it.

## Comparing the two approaches

| Question | Separate vendors | One connected team |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who coordinates the work? | You do | Your named contact |
| How many agreements? | One per vendor | One |
| Is the brand consistent? | Depends on each vendor | Set once, applied everywhere |
| Who fixes problems after launch? | Often unclear | The same team, under a Care plan |

## How to decide what is right for you

One team is not the right answer for every business. If you already have strong in-house design or a trusted developer, it may make sense to keep them and fill only the gaps. Ask these questions before you choose:

1. How much of my own time goes into coordinating suppliers each month?
2. Does my brand look the same everywhere a customer meets it?
3. When an enquiry arrives, does it reliably reach the right person?
4. Who will look after everything once it is live?

If the honest answers worry you, a single connected team is likely to save you money and time over the life of your online presence.
